Ice works for 20 minutes. Then someone has to start again.

After knee, shoulder or ankle surgery, your surgeon wants cold on the joint 4 to 6 times a day for the first week. With ice, that means melting packs, a wrap that slips, wet sheets — and a family member refilling it day and night.

By day three, most families are exhausted, and sessions get skipped just when they matter most.

Safety first

When not to use cold therapy

Check with your doctor first, and skip or pause it if you have:

  • Open wounds or unhealed incisions — until your surgeon says the wound is sealed
  • Raynaud's or strong cold sensitivity
  • Poor circulation — severe peripheral vascular disease
  • Reduced feeling in the area — diabetic neuropathy or nerve damage
  • Suspected DVT — leg swelling, redness or calf pain; get checked first
  • Sickle cell disease or cryoglobulinaemia
  • Young children — only under close supervision

Always keep the insulation barrier between the pad and your skin. Stop at once if the skin turns white, blue or grey, or the pain gets worse during a session.

Questions people ask us

How is Steady-Cold different from a cheaper cold compression machine?

Ask three questions of any unit: what temperature does the pad hold, how long does one fill last, and what pressure does it apply? Breg publishes all three for Polar Care. Compare on those, not on the photo.

When should I order?

3–5 days before surgery, so it's set up when you get home. If your surgery is already done, order now; the first week matters most.

Is it hard to set up?

Fill with ice and water, attach the pad, press start. We'll show you on a video call.

Can I sleep with it on?

Not routinely. Long, unbroken cold can harm skin. Use timed sessions unless your surgeon says otherwise.

Is ice good enough?

For a single sprain at home, often yes. For surgery recovery, where you need cold several times a day for two weeks, Steady-Cold holds the temperature and coverage that ice can't.

Does cold therapy slow healing?

Short, timed sessions help recovery by easing pain enough for earlier movement. Hours of non-stop cold may not — which is why sessions are timed.

Does it help knee osteoarthritis?

Less so. Cold can help during a sudden swollen flare. For daily OA pain, a Load-Shift brace has stronger evidence.
